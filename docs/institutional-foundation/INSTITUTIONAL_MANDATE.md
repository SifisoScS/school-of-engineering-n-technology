# Institutional Mandate

**Institution:** The School of Engineering & Technology  
**Version:** 0.2  
**Status:** Working draft — founding mandate for review; not an approved institutional policy  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-11-08  
**Related decisions:** D-001, D-002, D-003, D-004, D-005

## 1. Purpose

The proposed institution exists to develop people who can understand, investigate, design, build, operate and improve engineering and technological systems that contribute to South Africa's development.

Its founding educational approach combines academic knowledge, practical project-based learning, research and independent thinking. This integrated philosophy is recorded in `EDUCATIONAL_PHILOSOPHY.md` and reflects D-002.

## 2. Initial mandate

The initial institutional focus is the School of Engineering & Technology. Its proposed areas of study may include computing, software, artificial intelligence, electrical and electronic engineering, mechanical engineering, civil engineering, robotics, telecommunications, renewable energy and industrial engineering.

These areas are areas for investigation and programme development. They are not, by virtue of appearing in this document, approved departments, registered programmes or accredited qualifications.

The initial focus does not limit the wider long-term ambition recorded in D-005. The institution may consider a broader multidisciplinary education, research and innovation mandate over time, but any additional school or institute requires separate development, feasibility assessment and explicit approval.

## 3. South African relevance

The institution should identify and respond to engineering and technology needs in South Africa. Potential areas of public importance include energy, water, housing, transport, infrastructure, manufacturing, agriculture, digital systems and locally relevant innovation.

Programme priorities should be selected through evidence, consultation and feasibility assessment rather than assumption.

## 4. Intended contribution

The institution aims to contribute by:

- widening access to meaningful engineering and technology learning;
- connecting conceptual knowledge with practical application;
- developing research and problem-solving capabilities;
- encouraging responsible innovation;
- supporting the development of technical capability in South Africa; and
- building pathways for collaboration with relevant communities, industry and research organisations.

These are intended contributions, not claims of outcomes already achieved.

## 5. Learner access

The institution's access direction is recorded in D-004:

- foundational learning is intended to be broadly accessible;
- structured programmes may apply transparent, relevant and proportionate entry or progression requirements;
- any requirements must be appropriate to the learning being offered and must be communicated clearly; and
- regulated programmes and qualifications must follow applicable statutory, quality-assurance and accreditation requirements.

This mandate does not establish detailed admission, age, fee, bursary, recognition-of-prior-learning or learner-support policies. Those matters remain open for later design and approval.

## 6. Legal and operating direction

D-003 records a preferred hybrid commercial and public-benefit institutional direction. The exact legal and operating implementation remains undecided.

This mandate does not select a legal entity, establish public-benefit or tax status, approve an ownership or control structure, or authorise commercial or public-benefit operations. Any proposed structure requires qualified South African legal, tax, accounting and education-regulatory assessment.

## 7. Institutional boundaries

The institution must accurately distinguish between informal learning, non-credit-bearing education, certificates it may lawfully issue, and formal qualifications that require applicable registration, accreditation or other approvals.

It must not claim professional recognition, statutory authority or qualification-awarding status without verified authorisation. The long-term multidisciplinary ambition does not establish university status.

## 8. Scope not yet settled

The following matters remain open and must not be inferred from this mandate:

- the final legal and operating model;
- the institution's precise target learner groups;
- admission and progression requirements;
- the approved programme and qualification portfolio;
- accreditation and professional recognition pathways;
- the final school and department structure;
- the scope of future research activity;
- the relationship between the initial school and any future schools; and
- the funding, staffing, premises and delivery arrangements required for any future activity.

## 9. Phase and approval boundary

This document records a proposed founding mandate informed by D-001 through D-005. It is not a launch instruction, implementation plan, programme approval or authority to enrol learners.

Phase 0 was approved and closed by the founder on 8 November 2026 (see `PHASE_0_FOUNDER_APPROVAL.md`). No Phase 1 work or implementation is authorised by this document.

## 10. Review

This mandate should be reviewed alongside the Decision Register and the other Phase 0 foundation documents; the consolidated foundation was approved by the founder on 8 November 2026. Any later substantive change must be recorded through the institution's adopted decision-control process.
