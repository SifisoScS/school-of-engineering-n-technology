# Institutional Mandate

**Institution:** The School of Engineering & Technology  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Working draft — founding mandate for review

## 1. Purpose

The proposed institution exists to develop people who can understand, investigate, design, build, operate and improve engineering and technological systems that contribute to South Africa's development.

Its founding educational approach combines academic knowledge, practical project-based learning, research and independent thinking. This integrated philosophy is recorded in `EDUCATIONAL_PHILOSOPHY.md`.

## 2. Initial mandate

The initial institutional focus is the School of Engineering & Technology. Its proposed areas of study may include computing, software, artificial intelligence, electrical and electronic engineering, mechanical engineering, civil engineering, robotics, telecommunications, renewable energy and industrial engineering.

These areas are areas for investigation and programme development. They are not, by virtue of appearing in this document, approved departments, registered programmes or accredited qualifications.

## 3. South African relevance

The institution should identify and respond to engineering and technology needs in South Africa. Potential areas of public importance include energy, water, housing, transport, infrastructure, manufacturing, agriculture, digital systems and locally relevant innovation.

Programme priorities should be selected through evidence, consultation and feasibility assessment rather than assumption.

## 4. Intended contribution

The institution aims to contribute by:

- widening access to meaningful engineering and technology learning;
- connecting conceptual knowledge with practical application;
- developing research and problem-solving capabilities;
- encouraging responsible innovation;
- supporting the development of technical capability in South Africa; and
- building pathways for collaboration with relevant communities, industry and research organisations.

These are intended contributions, not claims of outcomes already achieved.

## 5. Institutional boundaries

The institution must accurately distinguish between informal learning, non-credit-bearing education, certificates it may lawfully issue, and formal qualifications that require applicable registration, accreditation or other approvals.

It must not claim professional recognition, statutory authority or qualification-awarding status without verified authorisation.

## 6. Scope not yet settled

The following matters remain open and must not be inferred from this mandate:

- the final legal and operating model;
- the institution's precise target learner groups;
- admission and progression requirements;
- the approved programme and qualification portfolio;
- accreditation and professional recognition pathways;
- the final school and department structure;
- the scope of future research activity; and
- the relationship between the initial school and any future schools.

## 7. Review

This mandate should be reviewed after the open Phase 0 decisions are resolved and before it is treated as an approved institutional policy.
