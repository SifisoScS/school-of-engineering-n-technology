# Governance Framework

**Institution:** The School of Engineering & Technology  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Design principles and questions — not an adopted governance constitution

## 1. Purpose

This document identifies the governance capabilities an independent educational institution should develop. It does not establish legal powers, appoint office-bearers or define a final board or management structure.

## 2. Governance objectives

The governance framework should protect:

- institutional independence and purpose;
- academic integrity and educational quality;
- lawful and responsible operation;
- transparent decision-making;
- financial and operational accountability;
- learner safety, dignity and fair treatment;
- privacy and responsible information handling; and
- effective management of conflicts of interest and institutional risk.

## 3. Governance principles

1. **Clear authority:** Every material decision should have an identified decision-maker.
2. **Accountability:** Authority should be accompanied by reporting and review.
3. **Separation of responsibilities:** Governance oversight, executive management and academic judgement should have appropriately defined roles.
4. **Academic integrity:** Academic decisions should be protected from improper influence.
5. **Transparency:** Policies, decisions and institutional claims should be accurate and appropriately documented.
6. **Fairness:** Learners, staff and stakeholders should have clear processes for complaints and review.
7. **Risk management:** Legal, financial, academic, safety, privacy and operational risks should be identified and monitored.
8. **Conflict management:** Actual, potential and perceived conflicts of interest should be disclosed and managed.

## 4. Governance functions to design

The institution should determine how the following functions will be performed:

- founder and ownership oversight;
- governing body or equivalent oversight;
- executive leadership;
- academic governance and programme quality;
- finance, audit and risk;
- learner protection, complaints and appeals;
- privacy, records and information security;
- health and safety for practical learning environments; and
- external partnerships and public accountability.

This list identifies functions, not a prescribed number of committees or offices.

## 5. Matters requiring legal and professional advice

Before adopting a governance structure, obtain appropriate South African legal and regulatory advice on:

- the legal entity and ownership arrangements;
- directors, trustees or other office-bearer duties, as applicable;
- education provider registration and accreditation obligations;
- employment and contracting;
- learner protection and consumer-facing representations;
- privacy and records management;
- occupational health and safety in workshops and laboratories;
- intellectual property; and
- financial reporting, tax and statutory obligations.

The applicable requirements depend on the eventual legal form and activities. This document is not legal advice.

## 6. Governance instruments to develop later

Potential instruments include:

- institutional constitution or founding governance instrument;
- delegations of authority;
- academic governance policy;
- conflict-of-interest policy;
- financial controls and procurement policy;
- risk management policy;
- complaints and appeals policy;
- learner protection and safeguarding policy;
- privacy and records policy;
- research ethics policy; and
- health and safety policy.

## 7. Open design questions

- What legal entity will own and operate the institution?
- What governing body is appropriate for that entity?
- How will founder authority and oversight be defined?
- Who will have final academic authority?
- How will independent review and accountability work?
- What representation, if any, should learners and external experts have?
- How will governance evolve when the institution opens a physical campus or additional schools?

## 8. Approval status

No governance body, office or authority is established merely by appearing in this draft. The final framework must be legally reviewed and formally adopted through the institution's approved founding process.
