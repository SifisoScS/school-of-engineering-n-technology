# Governance Framework

**Institution:** The School of Engineering & Technology  
**Version:** 0.2  
**Status:** Design principles and questions — not an adopted governance constitution  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-11-08  
**Related decisions:** D-001, D-002, D-003, D-004, D-005

## 1. Purpose

This document identifies governance capabilities that the proposed independent educational institution may need to develop. It does not establish legal powers, appoint office-bearers or define a final board or management structure.

The framework is a planning document only. No governance body, office, delegation or authority is created by its inclusion in this repository.

## 2. Founding directions that governance must respect

Any future governance design must preserve the confirmed founding directions:

- **D-001 — Independence:** protect the institution's independent founding identity and purpose.
- **D-002 — Educational philosophy:** protect the integration of academic knowledge, practical project-based learning, research and independent thinking.
- **D-003 — Hybrid direction:** assess the implications of the selected commercial and public-benefit direction without assuming a legal form, entity count or tax status.
- **D-004 — Learner access:** support broadly accessible foundational learning while permitting transparent, relevant and proportionate requirements for structured learning.
- **D-005 — Institutional ambition:** allow for possible long-term multidisciplinary development without treating future schools, a campus or a research institute as approved.

These directions constrain future design. They do not themselves create governance institutions or implementation authority.

## 3. Governance objectives

The future governance framework should protect:

- institutional independence and purpose;
- academic integrity and educational quality;
- lawful and responsible operation;
- transparent decision-making;
- financial and operational accountability;
- learner safety, dignity and fair treatment;
- privacy and responsible information handling; and
- effective management of conflicts of interest and institutional risk.

## 4. Governance principles

1. **Clear authority:** Every material decision should have an identified decision-maker.
2. **Accountability:** Authority should be accompanied by reporting and review.
3. **Separation of responsibilities:** Governance oversight, executive management and academic judgement should have appropriately defined roles.
4. **Academic integrity:** Academic decisions should be protected from improper influence.
5. **Transparency:** Policies, decisions and institutional claims should be accurate and appropriately documented.
6. **Fairness:** Learners, staff and stakeholders should have clear processes for complaints and review.
7. **Risk management:** Legal, financial, academic, safety, privacy and operational risks should be identified and monitored.
8. **Conflict management:** Actual, potential and perceived conflicts of interest should be disclosed and managed.

## 5. Governance functions to design

The institution would need to determine how the following functions should be performed, if and when the relevant operations are proposed:

- founder and ownership oversight;
- governing body or equivalent oversight;
- executive leadership;
- academic governance and programme quality;
- finance, audit and risk;
- learner protection, complaints and appeals;
- privacy, records and information security;
- health and safety for practical learning environments; and
- external partnerships and public accountability.

This list identifies functions for design; it does not prescribe a number of committees or offices, nor does it imply that these functions are currently staffed or operational.

## 6. Matters requiring legal and professional advice

Before adopting a governance structure, obtain appropriate South African legal and regulatory advice on:

- the legal entity and ownership arrangements;
- directors, trustees or other office-bearer duties, as applicable;
- education-provider registration and accreditation obligations;
- employment and contracting;
- learner protection and consumer-facing representations;
- privacy and records management;
- occupational health and safety in workshops and laboratories;
- intellectual property; and
- financial reporting, tax and statutory obligations.

The applicable requirements depend on the eventual legal form and activities. This document is not legal advice.

## 7. Governance instruments to consider later

Potential instruments include:

- institutional constitution or founding governance instrument;
- delegations of authority;
- academic governance policy;
- conflict-of-interest policy;
- financial controls and procurement policy;
- risk management policy;
- complaints and appeals policy;
- learner protection and safeguarding policy;
- privacy and records policy;
- research ethics policy; and
- health and safety policy.

These are possible future instruments, not existing or adopted policies.

## 8. Open design questions

- What legal entity will own and operate the institution?
- What governing body is appropriate for that entity?
- How will founder authority and oversight be defined?
- Who will have final academic authority?
- How will independent review and accountability work?
- What representation, if any, should learners and external experts have?
- How will governance evolve if a physical campus or additional schools are later approved?
- How will the governance arrangements protect the access principles in D-004?
- How will academic independence and the integrated educational philosophy in D-002 be protected?

## 9. Approval status and phase boundary

No governance body, office or authority is established merely by appearing in this draft. No founding process is represented as approved by this document.

The framework remains a design proposal. It requires further development, appropriate South African legal and professional review, and explicit founder consideration. Any formal governance arrangements must be established through the legally appropriate process after the relevant decisions have been made.

Phase 0 was approved and closed by the founder on 8 November 2026 (see `PHASE_0_FOUNDER_APPROVAL.md`). This document does not authorise Phase 1, incorporation, appointments, expenditure or institutional operations.
