# D-003 — Legal and Operating Model Decision

**Institutional reference:** The School of Engineering & Technology and the wider proposed institution described by the Phase 0 founding documents  
**Decision ID:** D-003  
**Version:** 0.2  
**Status:** Direction confirmed; legal implementation remains open  
**Phase:** 0 — Institutional Foundation  
**Decision provenance:** Founder-selected direction recorded in the Phase 0 Decision Register  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-11-08  
**Related decisions:** D-001, D-002, D-004, D-005

## 1. Decision

The selected direction is:

**C — Hybrid commercial and public-benefit institution.**

The wider proposed institution is intended to combine a financially sustainable operating model with a clearly defined public-benefit purpose. The hybrid direction is selected as the preferred institutional model for further design.

This direction applies to the proposed institution as a whole; it does not decide which legal entity or entities, if any, will carry out particular functions.

## 2. What this decision establishes

This decision establishes the desired direction, not a final legal structure.

The institution should investigate how its educational activities could be financially sustainable while also supporting public-benefit activities such as accessible learning resources, outreach, scholarships or other educational support, where feasible and lawful.

The assessment must preserve:

- the institutional independence recorded in D-001;
- the integrated educational philosophy recorded in D-002;
- the learner-access direction recorded in D-004; and
- the conditional, long-term multidisciplinary ambition recorded in D-005.

These are design constraints for future assessment, not authority to implement a particular structure or activity.

## 3. What this decision does not establish

D-003 does not:

- select or incorporate a particular company, non-profit company, trust, association or other legal entity;
- establish that multiple legal entities must be created;
- determine ownership, shareholding, membership or control;
- determine tax status or eligibility for any tax exemption;
- confer public-benefit organisation status or approval;
- authorise the institution to offer accredited qualifications;
- approve a funding, revenue-sharing or asset-transfer arrangement;
- authorise expenditure, incorporation or the commencement of operations; or
- replace professional legal, tax, accounting or education-regulatory advice.

A hybrid operating model may be achievable through one entity with appropriately governed activities, or through more than one legally distinct entity. Neither structure is selected by this decision.

## 4. Design requirements for a future legal review

Any future legal and operating model assessment should address:

1. institutional control and protection of the founding purpose;
2. the distinction between commercial activities and public-benefit activities;
3. governance duties, conflicts of interest and related-party transactions;
4. how funds, assets, intellectual property and staff may be shared or licensed;
5. financial reporting, audit and internal controls;
6. tax treatment and any relevant registration or approval requirements;
7. education-provider registration, accreditation and qualification obligations;
8. learner protection, privacy, consumer-facing representations and complaints;
9. sustainability, reinvestment and the use of any surplus; and
10. closure, restructuring and protection of learners if an operating entity fails.

The assessment must also consider how the institution's independence, educational philosophy and learner-access direction would be protected under each viable option.

## 5. Rationale

The hybrid direction is intended to avoid treating financial sustainability and public access as mutually exclusive.

A commercial operating capability may support continuity, investment and service delivery. A defined public-benefit function may support educational access and activities whose value is not measured solely by revenue.

These intended benefits depend on a lawful structure, transparent governance, viable finances and clear separation of responsibilities. They should not be assumed to arise automatically from choosing a hybrid model.

## 6. Required safeguards

Before any implementation is proposed, the institution should obtain South African professional advice and document:

- the recommended legal structure and reasons for it;
- the purpose and permitted activities of each entity, if more than one is proposed;
- governance and decision-making arrangements;
- financial separation and controls;
- agreements between related entities;
- tax and regulatory implications; and
- how the institution will communicate its legal and educational status accurately.

No public-benefit, tax-exempt, accreditation or qualification-awarding claim should be made unless the relevant status has been formally confirmed.

## 7. Proposed follow-up assessments — not implementation instructions

The following are possible assessment tasks for a later, explicitly authorised planning stage:

- obtain a South African legal and tax structure assessment;
- compare a single-entity model with a multi-entity model;
- assess whether any public-benefit registration or approval is appropriate and feasible;
- prepare a proposed entity and governance diagram for professional review; and
- present any recommended structure to the founder for an explicit decision.

Listing these assessments does not authorise commissioning advisers, incurring costs, forming entities or implementing a structure.
