# Institutional Evolution

**Institution:** The School of Engineering & Technology  
**Version:** 0.3  
**Status:** Long-term direction — indicative, not an approved delivery or implementation plan  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-11-08  
**Related decisions:** D-001, D-002, D-003, D-004, D-005

## 1. Vision

The institution is intended to begin with a digital-first focus on the School of Engineering & Technology and, over time, may develop into a broader multidisciplinary education, research and innovation institution. Its long-term possibilities may include a physical campus and additional schools.

This is a directional vision, not an approved sequence of projects, a funded plan or an implementation mandate.

## 2. Possible early direction — digital-first Engineering & Technology focus

The following are areas that may be investigated if and when the founder authorises subsequent planning:

- institutional identity and foundational policy development;
- public website development;
- design of online and offline learning resources;
- validation of learner needs and access conditions;
- development and testing of learning resources;
- design of academic quality processes; and
- investigation of partnerships and regulatory pathways.

This list is exploratory. It is not an approved Phase 1 work plan, delivery commitment or instruction to begin implementation.

The repository landing page has been described as a UI-only starting point, not a functioning learning management system. This is a repository-status statement associated with the reviewed project baseline; it should be rechecked before being repeated as a current technical status.

## 3. Possible structured learning and practical capability

Potential future development may include scoped learning pathways, practical projects and simulations, educator and learner-support processes, access to suitable practical facilities, evaluation of learning outcomes and sustainable operating arrangements.

Any such work would need separate scope, approval, resources and appropriate regulatory assessment. Regulated programmes must follow applicable pathways before being represented as accredited or recognised.

The learner-access direction in D-004 should inform future design: foundational learning is intended to be broadly accessible, while structured programmes may use transparent, relevant and proportionate entry or progression requirements.

## 4. Possible research and institutional partnerships

Potential future development may include research capability, collaboration with universities, industry, public institutions and communities, applied research addressing South African needs, research ethics and intellectual property processes, and evidence-based impact evaluation.

Research activity must match actual capacity, competent supervision, ethical oversight and applicable approvals. No research institute or formal research structure has been approved.

## 5. Possible physical campus

A physical campus is a long-term possibility only. If considered in a future authorised planning process, it may involve teaching and collaboration spaces, engineering workshops, appropriately equipped laboratories, research and prototyping facilities, learner support and library resources, and suitable safety and accessibility infrastructure.

Any campus proposal would require feasibility work covering location, premises, funding, infrastructure, staffing, safety, regulation and operating costs. No location, opening date, size or funding commitment has been selected.

## 6. Possible additional schools

The long-term ambition in D-005 is multidisciplinary. The first intended school remains the School of Engineering & Technology.

A **School of Politics & Governance** has been identified as a possible future school. Potential areas may include political science, public administration, constitutional democracy, public policy, local government, public finance, international relations, leadership and institutional governance.

This is exploratory. Its mandate, academic scope, governance, staffing, funding, programme approvals and timing require separate development, feasibility assessment and explicit approval. No additional school is approved.

## 7. Conditions for any future stage

If a later phase or stage is proposed, progression should depend on evidence rather than aspiration. Any proposed stage should have documented entry criteria, resources, risks, accountable owners and exit criteria.

Relevant considerations may include demonstrated learner need, educational quality, accurate and accessible resources, qualified people and facilities, legal compliance, sustainable funding, learner safety and evaluation of preceding work.

These are proposed planning criteria, not an approved stage-gate process.

## 8. Principles for possible expansion

1. Do not expand academic scope faster than quality systems can support.
2. Do not make accreditation or qualification claims without verification.
3. Do not treat a website launch as proof of an operating school.
4. Do not establish facilities without safety, financial and operational planning.
5. Preserve offline access as a core design consideration.
6. Evaluate any authorised activity and record lessons.
7. Require explicit institutional approval before establishing another school.

## 9. Open implementation decisions

No target dates or budgets, campus location, additional school launch, research institute structure, funding arrangements, partnership strategy or expansion authority have been approved.

The legal and operating direction in D-003 remains subject to professional assessment. Any future operating model must preserve the institution's independence and educational philosophy and must be designed consistently with the learner-access direction.

## 10. Phase and approval status

This document records the long-term direction selected in D-005. It is not a funded project plan, construction commitment, delivery mandate or approval to establish additional schools.

Phase 0 was approved and closed by the founder on 8 November 2026 (see `PHASE_0_FOUNDER_APPROVAL.md`). No Phase 1 or implementation plan has been approved. No activity in this document should be treated as authorised work unless separately approved.
