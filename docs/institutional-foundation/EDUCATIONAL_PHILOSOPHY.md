# Educational Philosophy

**Institution:** The School of Engineering & Technology  
**Version:** 0.2  
**Status:** Foundational direction confirmed; detailed framework to be developed  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-11-08  
**Approval authority:** Founder — consolidated Phase 0 foundation approved 2026-11-08 (Outcome A; see `PHASE_0_FOUNDER_APPROVAL.md`)  
**Related decisions:** D-002, D-004

## 1. Selected approach

The institution will adopt an integrated educational approach combining:

1. academic knowledge;
2. practical, project-based learning;
3. research and investigation; and
4. independent thinking.

These elements should reinforce one another rather than operate as separate or competing models.

This is the selected educational direction recorded in D-002. It is a founding philosophy, not approval to launch a programme, establish an academic department, enrol learners or begin Phase 1.

## 2. Academic knowledge

Learners need sound conceptual foundations. Programmes should develop understanding of relevant principles, mathematics, scientific methods, terminology, history and theory.

Academic depth should not be sacrificed for quick demonstrations or superficial project completion.

## 3. Practical application

Learners should have opportunities to apply concepts through projects, experiments, design exercises, simulations, construction, testing, maintenance and evaluation, as appropriate to the subject.

Practical work should have clear learning objectives and suitable safety controls. A project is not educational merely because something has been made; learners should be able to explain the principles, decisions, limitations and results.

## 4. Research and investigation

Learners should develop the ability to ask useful questions, identify reliable evidence, investigate problems, test hypotheses, analyse results and communicate conclusions.

Research should be introduced at an appropriate level and developed progressively.

## 5. Independent thinking

The institution should cultivate intellectual curiosity, disciplined reasoning, creativity and the ability to assess claims.

Learners should be encouraged to:

- ask questions and challenge assumptions respectfully;
- distinguish evidence from opinion;
- compare alternative explanations and designs;
- recognise uncertainty and limitations;
- explain and defend decisions; and
- revise conclusions when evidence changes.

Independent thinking does not mean rejecting established knowledge. It means engaging with knowledge actively and responsibly.

## 6. Connecting the four elements

A typical learning cycle may follow this pattern:

1. **Understand:** study the underlying concepts and principles.
2. **Investigate:** identify a question or real-world problem and examine available evidence.
3. **Design:** develop and justify a possible approach.
4. **Build or test:** apply the approach through practical work or simulation.
5. **Evaluate:** assess results, limitations, safety and consequences.
6. **Reflect and communicate:** explain what was learned and what should change.

This is a proposed learning pattern, not a prescribed assessment model for every subject.

## 7. South African context

Learning should engage with relevant South African challenges and opportunities, including infrastructure, energy, water, housing, transport, manufacturing, digital access and local innovation.

Local relevance should strengthen learning, not replace rigorous academic standards.

## 8. Digital and offline access

The educational model should support learning in environments where connectivity is inconsistent or expensive. Learning materials should be designed for accessible online use and, where technically appropriate, offline use.

These are design intentions. They do not claim that a platform or offline learning capability has already been implemented or verified.

## 9. Learner access

The educational philosophy must be applied consistently with D-004, the selected layered learner-access direction:

- foundational learning resources are intended to be broadly accessible;
- structured programmes may apply transparent, relevant and proportionate entry and progression requirements;
- advanced or specialist learning may require demonstrated prior knowledge, competence or experience; and
- regulated programmes and qualifications must follow applicable statutory and accreditation requirements.

This philosophy does not establish detailed admissions, age, fee, bursary, recognition-of-prior-learning or learner-support policies. Those matters remain open for separate design and approval.

## 10. Matters still to be designed

This philosophy does not yet define:

- learner age groups or admission criteria;
- programme levels and prerequisites;
- curriculum standards or qualification alignment;
- teaching and assessment methods for each discipline;
- educator qualifications and responsibilities;
- laboratory and workshop standards; or
- accreditation and professional recognition pathways.

These require separate academic and regulatory work.

## 11. Approval and phase boundary

D-002 confirms the educational direction only. This document is not a programme approval, curriculum approval, assessment policy, operational instruction or authority to enrol learners.

Phase 0 was approved and closed by the founder on 8 November 2026 (see `PHASE_0_FOUNDER_APPROVAL.md`). This philosophy does not authorise Phase 1 or implementation.
