**Scope:** The fifteen existing institutional-foundation documents and repository-root `README.md` at the reviewed baseline (sixteen documents); this review is an additional document.

## Review outcome

The five recorded founding directions form a coherent institutional baseline. The package describes an independent privately founded South African institution, an integrated educational philosophy, a hybrid commercial/public-benefit direction, layered learner access, and a multidisciplinary long-term ambition that begins with Engineering & Technology. D-003 does not select a legal entity or implementation. D-004 and D-005 do not approve detailed admissions policies or additional schools. None of the five decisions authorises Phase 1.

The package is not yet ready to be described as finally approved. During this review, I corrected several concrete inconsistencies: the charter now reflects D-003 and D-004 without settling implementation; the open-decisions file no longer says approval has already occurred; completed D-004/D-005 integration and review checklist items are marked complete; the academic-architecture text no longer implies a school structure was approved; and the repository-root README now refers to D-001 through D-005. The remaining issues below should be resolved or explicitly accepted before the founder approves the package.

## Decision baseline

- **D-001 — Institutional independence:** independent, privately founded South African institution.
- **D-002 — Educational philosophy:** integrated academic knowledge, practical project-based learning, research, and independent thinking.
- **D-003 — Legal and operating direction:** hybrid commercial and public-benefit direction; legal form, entity count, ownership/control, tax position, and implementation remain undecided.
- **D-004 — Learner access:** foundational learning is intended to be broadly accessible; structured learning may use transparent, relevant, proportionate requirements. Detailed policy remains open.
- **D-005 — Institutional ambition:** broader multidisciplinary education, research, and innovation institution over time; Engineering & Technology first; Politics & Governance remains a possible future school requiring separate approval.

These are founding directions, not evidence that the institution is registered, operating, accredited, authorised to offer regulated qualifications, or ready to enrol learners.

## South African regulatory and professional advice

The following points are issue-spotting for qualified South African advisers, not legal conclusions about this proposed institution.

**Education-provider and qualification route.** The applicable route depends on what will actually be offered: informal resources, non-credit learning, occupational training, or a qualification under a particular NQF sub-framework. The government’s private higher-education service describes DHET registration and programme-level accreditation/registration requirements for private higher-education institutions. SAQA describes the three NQF sub-frameworks and the respective quality councils, and explains the separate roles of provider accreditation and qualification registration.[1] [2] Before publishing an offer, obtain advice from DHET and the relevant quality council on the exact provider category, programme type, registration, accreditation, qualification registration, advertising, student-protection, and teach-out requirements. Do not assume that an online or short course is automatically outside regulation or that a non-credit course is an NQF qualification.

**Engineering recognition.** ECSA states that programme accreditation concerns educational criteria relevant to eligibility to apply for registration in a corresponding professional category.[3] If the institution later intends to offer engineering programmes or make professional-pathway claims, seek ECSA advice on the relevant programme category, accreditation process, facilities, educator capacity, safety, assessment, and wording of public claims. Listing an engineering subject area does not itself confer programme accreditation or professional registration.

**Entity, tax, and public-benefit status.** CIPC distinguishes a non-profit company legal form and its governing-document/non-distribution requirements.[4] SARS approval as a public benefit organisation is a separate tax determination under the Income Tax Act; section 18A donation-receipt approval is also distinct, and some trading or business receipts may remain taxable.[5] Department of Social Development describes NPO registration as a voluntary registration facility with its own reporting obligations; it is not equivalent to incorporation or SARS PBO approval.[6] Obtain South African legal and tax advice comparing viable single- and multiple-entity options, governance and fiduciary control, related-party dealings, public-benefit tests, trading activities, VAT and income-tax treatment, grants and donations, asset/IP sharing, reporting, dissolution, and the cost of ongoing compliance. No option is recommended or selected by this review.

**Learner-facing services and information.** Before enrolment or paid services, obtain advice on consumer-facing representations and contracts, including fees, service scope, cancellation/refund terms, complaints, and marketing. The Consumer Protection Act’s stated purposes include fair markets, improved consumer information, and prohibiting certain unfair marketing and business practices.[7] Any future learning platform should also be assessed under POPIA for lawful processing, notices, retention, security, processor arrangements, access/correction requests, breach response, and children’s information if children may be learners. POPIA gives data subjects rights and imposes additional conditions on processing children’s information.[8] These considerations do not imply that the current UI-only website processes learner records.

**Operations and safeguarding.** Before physical workshops, laboratories, or a campus are proposed for approval, obtain advice on occupational health and safety, premises, equipment, supervision, insurance, safeguarding, employment and contractor arrangements, and required local or sector approvals. Confirm the appropriate legal and regulatory advice based on the actual activities; this review does not prescribe an operating structure.
