## 1. Purpose and authorisation status

Phase 0 answered the question: *What institution are we establishing, and what principles will guide it?*

Phase 1 is proposed to answer a different question: *What will it take to establish and operate this institution successfully in South Africa?*

Phase 1 is proposed as a research, feasibility and institutional-design stage. It is not a launch or implementation stage. Its primary outcome would be an evidence-based institutional establishment blueprint, with findings, recommendations, assumptions and unresolved decisions clearly distinguished.

**This plan is not authorisation to execute Phase 1.** Phase 1 may begin only when the founder has explicitly selected either "Authorise" or "Authorise with changes" in Section 12, completed the authorisation record, and confirmed any applicable conditions. Selecting "Do not authorise" does not authorise execution. Until an affirmative authorisation is recorded, Phase 1 remains unauthorised and no work described in this plan may begin.

The approved Phase 0 foundation in [`docs/institutional-foundation/`](../institutional-foundation/README.md) remains the controlling baseline. This plan does not change D-001 through D-005 or any Phase 0 document.

## 2. Objectives

By the end of Phase 1, the institution should understand:

1. what legal and regulatory pathway applies to the proposed institution;
2. what educational offerings it can realistically begin with;
3. what academic structure and expertise it needs;
4. who its prospective learners are and what they need;
5. how its online and offline delivery model should work;
6. what governance and operational capabilities are required;
7. what financial and infrastructure resources are needed; and
8. whether the institution is feasible, and what should happen next.

## 3. Workstreams

Seven connected areas of investigation are proposed.

### 01 — Legal and Regulatory Feasibility

**Scope:** legal-entity and operating-structure options; South African education regulatory pathways; registration, accreditation and qualification requirements; intellectual property, data protection and learner safeguarding.

**Key questions:** What can lawfully be established and offered, under which framework? Which offerings require registration or accreditation, and which do not? What must professional advisers confirm?

**Builds on:** `D-003_HYBRID_MODEL_DECISION.md`, `LEGAL_AND_OPERATING_MODEL.md`, the regulatory issue-spotting in `PHASE_0_FINAL_REVIEW.md`.

**Deliverable:** Legal and Regulatory Pathway Report.

### 02 — Academic Architecture

**Scope:** initial engineering and technology disciplines; foundational versus advanced learning; academic standards and assessment philosophy; research and project-based learning; qualification versus non-qualification offerings.

**Key questions:** What can the institution legitimately teach and offer at its initial stage? Which offerings are realistic without accreditation?

**Builds on:** `ACADEMIC_ARCHITECTURE.md`, `EDUCATIONAL_PHILOSOPHY.md`, `D-004_LEARNER_ACCESS_DECISION.md`.

**Deliverable:** Academic Development Blueprint.

### 03 — Learner and Societal Needs

**Scope:** South African education and skills needs; prospective learner groups; digital access and connectivity barriers; language, accessibility and affordability; employer and community needs.

**Key questions:** Who would the institution serve first, and what evidence supports that choice?

**Research quality:** Research must distinguish between desk-based evidence, direct engagement with prospective learners and employers, and assumptions that have not been validated. Estimated demand must not be presented as demonstrated demand. This requirement does not itself authorise direct engagement; Section 8 governs external engagement.

**Builds on:** `ACCESSIBILITY_AND_INCLUSION.md`, `INSTITUTIONAL_MANDATE.md`.

**Deliverable:** Learner and Societal Needs Assessment.

### 04 — Digital and Physical Delivery Model

**Scope:** digital-first institutional architecture; online and offline learning requirements; learning-platform requirements; future workshops, laboratories and facilities; accessibility and device compatibility.

**Key questions:** What delivery capability is required for the offerings identified in Workstreams 01 and 02, and what is the minimum viable approach?

**Builds on:** `ACCESSIBILITY_AND_INCLUSION.md`, `INSTITUTIONAL_EVOLUTION.md`, `FOUNDING_CHARTER.md`.

**Deliverable:** Delivery and Technology Architecture. This is a requirements and architecture document. It is not a platform build.

### 05 — Governance and Operating Model

**Scope:** institutional accountability; academic governance; management and operational responsibilities; the relationship between commercial and public-benefit activity; policies, controls and institutional records.

**Key questions:** What governance is required for the legal and operating options identified in Workstream 01?

**Builds on:** `GOVERNANCE_FRAMEWORK.md`, `LEGAL_AND_OPERATING_MODEL.md`, `DOCUMENT_CONTROL_STANDARD.md` (still a proposed convention).

**Deliverable:** Governance and Operating Model Proposal.

### 06 — Financial and Resource Feasibility

**Scope:** establishment costs; operational expenditure; funding possibilities; sustainable revenue models; staffing and infrastructure requirements; financial risks and scenarios.

**Key questions:** What is the minimum viable institutional model, and how could it sustain itself?

**Builds on:** `D-003_HYBRID_MODEL_DECISION.md`, `LEGAL_AND_OPERATING_MODEL.md`.

**Deliverable:** Financial Feasibility Assessment.

### 07 — Institutional Establishment Roadmap

**Scope:** consolidating research findings; identifying dependencies; setting out implementation options; prioritising institutional capabilities; identifying risks and unresolved decisions.

**Builds on:** the outputs of Workstreams 01–06.

**Deliverable:** Institutional Establishment Blueprint.

## 4. Priority questions

Three questions take priority, because they constrain nearly every other design decision:

1. **Legal feasibility:** What exactly can be established, and under which South African regulatory framework?
2. **Academic feasibility:** What can the institution legitimately teach and offer at its initial stage?
3. **Financial feasibility:** What is the minimum viable institutional model, and how can it sustain itself?

No resources should be spent on building a digital platform before it is understood which educational services the institution is legally and practically equipped to deliver.

## 6. Research standards

### Evidence classification

Every finding must be classified as one of:

| Class | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Verified** | Supported by reliable evidence, with the source cited. |
| **Proposed** | A recommended institutional direction. |
| **Assumption** | Not yet verified. |
| **Decision required** | Requires founder approval. |

This classification is intended to prevent assumptions from quietly becoming institutional policy.

A finding classified as Verified represents a finding supported by reliable evidence. It does not automatically mean that the institution has approved or adopted the finding as policy or an institutional decision.

### Sources

- Prefer primary South African official sources, for example DHET, SAQA, CHE, QCTO, ECSA, CIPC, SARS, the Department of Social Development and the Information Regulator, and the legislation they administer.
- Cite each source with its title, URL or reference, and the date it was accessed. Regulatory information changes; undated findings should not be relied on.
- Where legislation, regulations or official guidance are relied upon, record the specific provision or section, its applicable version or effective date, and any relevant amendments. Where official sources conflict or appear outdated, record the discrepancy rather than silently selecting one interpretation.
- Treat secondary sources (news, commentary, other institutions' practice) as context only. Do not use them to verify a regulatory requirement.

### Research and Evidence Register

Findings are recorded in a register with these fields:

1. ID;
2. finding;
3. evidence class;
4. source;
5. source date or version;
6. specific reference, such as a legislative section, regulation, or official document page or paragraph;
7. date accessed;
8. workstream;
9. limitations or uncertainty; and
10. whether professional confirmation is needed.

### Limits of the research

Phase 1 research may be carried out by an AI research agent. Its findings are not legal, tax, accounting or regulatory advice. Any finding that would be relied on to form an entity, make an application or make a public claim must be flagged for confirmation by a qualified South African professional or by the relevant authority.

## 8. Boundaries — what Phase 1 must not do

There is an important distinction between designing an institution and establishing one. Even if Phase 1 is authorised, it must not:

- register companies, trusts or other entities;
- submit accreditation or registration applications;
- recruit staff or appoint academic leadership;
- enrol learners or launch courses;
- build the full learning management system;
- purchase property or establish a campus;
- establish laboratories or workshops;
- launch additional schools;
- commit the institution to expenditure or funding arrangements;
- commission professional advisers or contact regulators without a specific founder approval for each such action; or
- make public claims, or change the landing page, on the basis of Phase 1 findings without founder approval.

These activities require separate decisions and authorisations.

### External research engagement

No surveys, interviews, consultations, requests for information or other direct engagement with prospective learners, employers, educational institutions, community organisations or other external parties may be conducted without specific founder approval. Any approved engagement must have an agreed purpose, scope, method and appropriate privacy and safeguarding arrangements.

This restriction applies even after Phase 1 is authorised. Desk-based research remains within the proposed scope.

## 9. Approval gates

| Gate | When | Founder decision |
|---|---|---|
| **Gate 0** | Before any Phase 1 work | Authorise execution of this plan, with or without changes (Section 12). |
| **Gate 1** | End of Stage 1 | Accept the Research and Evidence Register and confirm Stage 2 may proceed. |
| **Gate 2** | End of Stage 2 | Accept the workstream reports and confirm Stage 3 may proceed. |
| **Gate 3** | End of Stage 3 | Accept the draft Institutional Establishment Blueprint for final assessment. |
| **Gate 4** | End of Stage 4 | Choose one of the options below. |

### Gate 4 decision options

- **Option A — Proceed to establishment planning.** The institution is sufficiently feasible to develop a detailed establishment and implementation proposal.
- **Option B — Proceed with conditions.** Certain legal, financial, academic or operational questions must be resolved first.
- **Option C — Redesign the institutional model.** The findings show that important parts of the proposed model need reconsideration.
- **Option D — Pause further development.** The institution is not ready to proceed.

None of these options by itself authorises implementation. Any subsequent phase requires its own proposal and separate founder authorisation.

### Gate rule

No approval gate is passed automatically. The founder must explicitly record the decision at each gate before the next stage may begin. Acceptance of a deliverable does not automatically constitute approval of every recommendation contained within it.

## 10. Completion criteria

Phase 1 is complete only when the following evidence exists:

| Requirement | Expected evidence |
|---|---|
| Legal feasibility | Verified regulatory pathway and professional-advice requirements |
| Academic feasibility | Proposed initial academic architecture |
| Learner demand | Evidence-based needs assessment |
| Technology | Documented delivery architecture and requirements |
| Governance | Proposed operating and accountability model |
| Financial viability | Cost estimates and sustainability scenarios |
| Establishment strategy | Prioritised roadmap with dependencies and risks |

**Completion does not imply viability or approval.** Phase 1 is complete when the required investigations and deliverables have been produced, their evidence and limitations have been documented, and unresolved questions have been identified. Findings may demonstrate feasibility, conditional feasibility, infeasibility or insufficient evidence. A negative or inconclusive finding does not prevent Phase 1 from being completed.

### Role and authority of the execution agent

Claude acts as a research and documentation agent under the founder's direction. Its authority is limited to activities explicitly authorised under this plan. It may investigate, analyse, organise evidence and prepare recommendations, but may not make institutional decisions, approve its own recommendations, create external commitments, incur expenditure, or authorise progression between stages. All founder decisions and approvals remain subject to explicit founder confirmation.
