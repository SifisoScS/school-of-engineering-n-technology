# Founding Charter

**Institution:** The School of Engineering & Technology  
**Jurisdictional focus:** South Africa  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Preliminary working draft — not yet ratified

## 1. Founding intent

The School of Engineering & Technology is being developed as an independent, privately founded South African educational institution. Its purpose is to develop people who can understand, design, build, investigate and improve the technologies, engineering systems and infrastructure that contribute to society.

The institution intends to begin as a digital-first school, providing learning through online and offline access. Its longer-term direction includes the development of physical facilities and the potential establishment of additional schools.

## 2. Institutional independence

The founding direction is institutional independence. The intended institution will develop its own identity, leadership and governance arrangements, and its own educational philosophy and academic framework.

Independence does not remove the need to comply with applicable South African laws and regulatory requirements. Partnerships may be pursued where they advance educational quality, research, practical experience or public benefit, without surrendering the institution's defined identity and governance responsibilities.

The precise legal entity and registration structure remain to be determined.

## 3. Educational commitment

The institution adopts an integrated educational approach that brings together:

- academic knowledge and disciplined study;
- practical, project-based application;
- research, investigation and experimentation; and
- independent, evidence-informed thinking.

Learning should connect theory with real problems. Students should be encouraged to explain their reasoning, test ideas, build and evaluate solutions, learn from failure and communicate their findings.

## 4. South African relevance

The institution's initial focus is South Africa. Its educational priorities should respond to the country's social, economic, technological and infrastructure realities, while maintaining an outward-looking perspective and the potential to serve learners elsewhere in Africa.

The institution should not assume that all learners have reliable internet access, expensive devices, prior tertiary education or the same educational background.

## 5. Digital-first development

The initial delivery model is intended to support both online and offline learning. The digital platform should be designed around low-bandwidth access, downloadable learning resources, local progress storage and synchronisation when connectivity is available.

The first website or landing page is not itself the learning platform. Any functionality must be described accurately according to what has actually been implemented.

## 6. Long-term institutional direction

The long-term vision is to grow from a digital-first school into a physical institution with suitable learning spaces, workshops, laboratories and research facilities.

A future School of Politics & Governance has been identified as a possible addition to the wider institution. Its mandate, relationship to the founding school and governance arrangements have not yet been approved.

## 7. Foundational principles

The following principles are proposed for further development and ratification:

1. **Integrity:** Make accurate claims about institutional status, learning outcomes and qualifications.
2. **Accessibility:** Reduce avoidable barriers to learning, including connectivity and device barriers.
3. **Practical application:** Connect knowledge to meaningful work and real-world problems.
4. **Intellectual independence:** Encourage curiosity, critical inquiry and evidence-based reasoning.
5. **South African relevance:** Address local needs without narrowing academic standards.
6. **Accountability:** Establish clear responsibilities, transparent decisions and appropriate oversight.
7. **Continuous improvement:** Evaluate learning quality and improve programmes using evidence.

## 8. Matters reserved for further decisions

This charter does not settle:

- the institution's final legal form or ownership arrangements;
- whether it will operate on a commercial, non-profit or hybrid basis;
- admission and access rules;
- the academic and qualification framework;
- accreditation, registration or professional recognition pathways;
- the final institutional structure and future schools;
- detailed governance powers and appointment processes; or
- the timing, location and funding of a physical campus.

These matters must be resolved through documented decisions and appropriate professional advice.

## 9. Regulatory integrity

The institution must distinguish between providing learning, issuing its own certificates of participation or completion, and offering qualifications that require statutory registration or accreditation.

No claim of accreditation, professional recognition, registration or authority to award recognised qualifications should be made unless it has been verified and formally obtained from the relevant South African authority.

## 10. Ratification

This is a preliminary founding document. It becomes a formal institutional charter only after the founders have reviewed and approved its contents and confirmed the appropriate legal and governance process.
